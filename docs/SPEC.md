# Plan format and validation (v1)

The plan format, the validator and the test plan generator come from planwalk, a public repository by the same author (MIT licence, described in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md`). A plan is a small JSON file. It is made from an image or a PDF of a floor plan (`docs/PROMPT.md` has a prompt for that), the validator checks it, and the page builds a model of the flat from it. The validator is an isomorphic ES module with no dependencies, no DOM and no network access.

## 1. Coordinate system and units

- Plan space is 2D: x to the right, y downward, exactly like an image. The origin is arbitrary.
- `units` is one of `m`, `cm`, `mm`, `ft`, `in` (default `m`). Every length in the file (coordinates, thickness, heights, offsets, sizes) is in those units, and a declared room area is in the same unit squared. `normalizePlan` in `src/core/defaults.js` converts everything to metres (factors in `src/core/units.js`: cm 0.01, mm 0.001, ft 0.3048, in 0.0254); all code after that works in metres.
- 3D world (three.js): metres, y up. A plan point `(x, y)` at height `h` above the floor of a level maps to world `(x, level.elevation + h, y)`, so world +z is plan +y ("down" on the plan).
- Angles are degrees, clockwise on the plan seen from above. In three.js that is `object.rotation.y = -degToRad(angle)`.

## 2. Plan format

The authoritative definition is `schema/plan.v1.schema.json` (JSON Schema 2020-12). `docs/plan-format.md` is a shorter guide with an example, and `examples/flat-1.plan.json` and `examples/duplex-1.plan.json` (two levels joined by a stair) are complete plans. Summary:

- Top level: `version` (1) and `levels[]` (1 to 12) are required. Optional: `name` (up to 120 characters), `units`, `meta`. `meta` holds `source` (`image`, `pdf`, `sketch`, `synthetic` or `manual`), `scale_note` (up to 1000 characters), `notes` (up to 2000) and `generator` (up to 200); other keys in `meta` are allowed. `scale_note` is where the reader says how it got the scale. The schema has no flag for a guessed scale and nothing measures the scale: the validator only checks that sizes look plausible (E011, W104, W107, W112 to W115) and that declared areas fit the polygons (W102).
- Ids (levels, walls, openings, rooms, stairs, furniture) are strings of letters, digits, `_`, `-` and `.`, 1 to 40 characters. An id is unique among the items of the same list on a level (E003), and level ids are unique across the plan.
- `levels[]`: `id` and `walls[]` (3 to 600) are required. Optional: `name` (up to 80 characters), `elevation`, `height` (default 2.7 m, above 0), `slab` (default 0.2 m), `openings[]`, `rooms[]`, `stairs[]`, `furniture[]`. `elevation` is the height of the floor above the ground. It defaults to the elevation of the previous level plus that level's `height` and `slab` (0 for the first level), so set it when a drawing shows only an upper floor.
- `walls[]`: `id`, `from [x, y]`, `to [x, y]`. Optional: `kind` (`exterior`, `interior` by default, `partition`), `thickness` (default by kind: exterior 0.30 m, interior 0.12 m, partition 0.08 m) and `height` (overrides the level height, for a parapet for example). A wall is its centerline plus thickness.
- `openings[]`: `id`, `wall` (id of a wall on the same level), `type` (`door`, `window`, `passage`), `offset` (distance along the wall from its `from` end to the near edge of the opening), `width`. Optional: `height` (door 2.05, window 1.2, passage 2.1 m) and `sill` (window 0.9, door and passage 0 m). Doors only: `hinge` (`start` by default, or `end`: the end of the opening that carries the hinge, `start` being the end near the wall's `from` point) and `opens_to` (`left` by default, or `right`: the side of the wall the leaf swings toward, looking from `from` to `to` on the plan seen from above with y down; on a wall drawn from north to south, `left` is the east side).
- `rooms[]`: `id` and `polygon` (3 to 120 points along the wall centerlines, closed implicitly, any orientation) are required. Rooms that are open to each other, with no wall between them, share an edge. Optional: `name` (up to 80 characters), `use` (`living`, `bedroom`, `kitchen`, `bathroom`, `hall`, `balcony`, `storage`, `office`, `dining`, `other`), `floor` (`wood` by default, `tile`, `carpet`, `stone`, `concrete`, `none`), `ceiling` (default true; false for an open balcony or terrace) and `area` (the declared area, usually printed on the drawing and net of the walls; the validator compares it with the polygon, see W102).
- `stairs[]`: `id`, `from`, `to`, `width` and `steps` (a whole number, 2 to 40) are required. `from` is the centre of the step line at this level's floor (the lowest step line when the stair goes up), `to` the centre of the step line at the far end. Optional: `to_level` (the level the far end reaches) and `rise` (only without `to_level`: the height from the first to the last step line, negative when the stair goes down from this level, never 0; default the level height plus the slab, upward). Without `to_level` the stair leaves the plan: use that on a drawing of one floor, where the other floor is not part of the file.
- `furniture[]`: `id`, `type` and `center` are required. Optional: `size [w, d]` (width along local x, depth along local y), `rotation` (degrees, default 0) and `height`. `type` is one of `bed-double`, `bed-single`, `sofa`, `armchair`, `table-dining`, `table-coffee`, `chair`, `desk`, `wardrobe`, `kitchen-counter`, `fridge`, `stove`, `sink`, `bathtub`, `shower`, `toilet`, `basin`, `tv-unit`, `bookshelf`, `plant`, `rug`; default sizes by type are `FURNITURE_DEFAULTS` in `src/model/geometry.js`. The format carries furniture and the validator checks it, but plandoll does not use it (`docs/PROMPT.md` calls it optional): the page clears the list and its layout engine furnishes the rooms itself (`src/app/main.js`, `docs/DESIGN.md`).
- A piece of furniture faces +y (down the plan) at `rotation: 0`: its front, doors, drawers and seat face that way and its back is at -y. Angles run clockwise seen from above, so 180 faces up the plan, 90 faces left (-x) and 270 faces right. A bed has its head at -x (left) at 0 and up the plan at 90.

## 3. Geometry semantics

`buildModel` in `src/model/geometry.js` turns a normalized plan (metres) into the model, and `src/render/scene.js` draws it. Both follow these rules.

1. Wall solid: a box along the centerline, `length x thickness x height`, base at the level elevation. When a wall end touches another wall (the end point lies within `0.5 * thickness + 0.02` m of another wall's centerline segment, not counting the wall itself), that end is extended by half the wall's own thickness, so corners and T junctions close without gaps.
2. Openings cut a gap in the wall interval `[offset, offset + width]` (measured from the wall's `from` end, before the end extension) between the heights `[sill, sill + height]`; the gap is clamped to the wall. The remaining solids are the full height pieces on both sides of the gap, a lintel above (from `sill + height` to the wall height) and, when `sill` is above 0 (windows by default), a piece below (0 to `sill`). A door gets a frame and a leaf: a thin box (4 cm) hinged at the `hinge` end and swung 75 degrees toward `opens_to`. A window gets a frame, a thin pane of glass (plus a mullion in the middle when it is wider than 1.5 m) and a sill board. A passage is a bare gap.
3. Floors and ceilings: a room whose `floor` is not `none` has a floor surface with its top face at the level elevation and a slab under it, the polygon extruded downward by the level `slab` (less 4 mm, so that faces do not coincide). Floors may run to the wall centerlines (hidden under the walls). A room with `ceiling` true (the default) has a ceiling at `elevation + height`; the page keeps ceilings hidden.
4. Stairs: the stair is `steps` boxes, `width` wide, spaced evenly from `from` to `to`. The first box is centred on the first step line and the last on the last one, and each is `run` deep, where `run` is the distance from `from` to `to` divided by `steps - 1`. Box `i` (counting from 0 at the lowest step line) rises to `(i + 1) * riser` above its starting floor, with `riser = delta / steps`. With `to_level`, `delta` is the elevation of the upper level minus that of the lower one, and the stair belongs to the lower level: the steps start at its floor and reach the floor of the upper level (when `to_level` is lower than the stair's own level, `from` and `to` are swapped for this, so that the first step line is at the lower floor). Without `to_level`, `delta` is the absolute value of `rise`, and the steps climb from this level's floor (or, for a negative `rise`, descend below it, `from` being the end at this level's floor). Where the whole stair footprint (the rectangle from `from` to `to`, extended by half a step depth at both ends, `width` wide) lies inside one room, the model cuts a hole in that room's ceiling on the lower level and in its floor on the upper level; for a stair without `to_level`, the hole is in the ceiling of this level's room, or in its floor for a stair that goes down. Otherwise the rooms of the upper level should not cover the footprint.

## 4. Validation (src/core/validate.js, tools/validate.mjs)

Interface (isomorphic ES module, no Node only imports):

```js
validatePlan(plan, { strict }?) -> { ok: boolean, errors: Issue[], warnings: Issue[], normalized: object | null }
analyzePlan(plan, { strict }?) -> { ok, errors, warnings, normalized, salvaged, dropped, skipped: Skipped[], partial: boolean, ignored: { path, key }[], repairs }
modelOf(result) -> object | null   // the plan a model can be built from: `normalized`, or `salvaged` when `partial`
Issue = { code: 'E008', path: 'levels[0].rooms[2]', id?: 'bath', message: 'what is wrong, with numbers', fix: 'what to change, written to be pasted back and fixed' }
Skipped = { kind: 'level' | 'wall' | 'opening' | 'room' | 'stair' | 'furniture', id?: string, path: 'levels[1].openings[8]', why: string, reason: 'invalid' | 'wall-skipped' | 'level-skipped' }
```

`plan` is an object or a text (see "Text input" below). `ok` is true when `errors` is empty. `normalized` is `normalizePlan(plan)` when nothing had to be left out of the plan, else `null`. Messages are concrete (ids, names, coordinates, values) and every issue has a `fix` sentence, because the output is meant to be pasted back and fixed.

**Tolerant reading.** A pasted answer is rarely perfect, so one stray property or one bad element does not make a plan unusable.
- A property the schema does not know (a `furniture` list inside a room, `notes`, `comment`) is warning W116: it is ignored and does not appear in `normalized` (`ignored` lists them), and a number that is not finite inside it is no error. `"$schema"` and keys that start with `x-` are allowed without a word. With `strict: true` (command line `--strict`) any other unknown property is error E002. A wrong type or value of a known property is always E002. The keys that every object has (`__proto__`, `constructor`, `toString`) stay errors in both modes.
- An element (a wall, opening, room, stair or furniture item) that breaks a schema rule the geometry depends on is left out: a wrong type, a missing required property, a number outside its range, a point that is not a pair of numbers, a number that is not finite (E010). A value outside an enum, an id with odd characters, a text over its length and the purely cosmetic properties (`name`, `use`, `floor`, `ceiling`, `area`, `kind`, `hinge`, `opens_to`) only get their error and the element stays. A level whose own properties break such a rule (for example a `height` of 0 or less) is left out whole. An opening on a wall that was left out is left out with it (`reason: 'wall-skipped'`), and so is a stair whose `to_level` names a level that was left out (`reason: 'level-skipped'`). A `units` that is not one of the listed values makes the whole plan unusable (every length would be read at the wrong scale): nothing is salvaged and the error names `units`.
- When something was left out, `normalized` is `null` and `ok` is false, `salvaged` is the plan without those elements, `skipped` lists them (kind, id, path, the first error of the element as `why`), `dropped` counts them, and `partial` is true when something was left out and `salvaged` still has a wall. `modelOf(result)` returns `normalized`, else `salvaged` when `partial`, else `null`. With no level or no wall left, with an array over its cap (E013), with an invalid `units`, or with an error that is not in an element (a number in `meta` that is not finite), there is no model: `partial` is false.

**Text input.** A string is read as text, so a whole pasted answer works (`src/core/extract-json.js`). Strict JSON is used as it is (a list that holds a plan gives the plan). Otherwise the validator looks in the fenced blocks that contain a `{` (the first 200), then in the whole text when no block held a plan. It drops a leading line that is just `json` and cuts out balanced top level objects (every one it finds, ignoring braces inside strings; it gives up on a text after three braces that never close or 500 objects that are not JSON). It prefers an object that has a `levels` key, and when several objects have one (an answer with a first try and a corrected version) it takes the last and says so in `repairs`. It repairs typographic quotes used as delimiters (typographic quotes inside a value are kept), `//` and `/* */` comments, trailing commas, zero width spaces and byte order marks (zero width joiners and non joiners stay inside strings, where emoji and Persian or Indic text need them) and non breaking spaces. The result carries `repairs`, a list of short English phrases for what was changed (empty for strict JSON), for example `took the code block`. Text over 2,000,000 characters (`src/core/limits.js`) is refused with E001, before any other work.

Error codes (a code is never reused):
- E001 not a JSON object, or unreadable text (including a text over the limit above); E002 schema violation (the message names the JSON pointer and the rule; an unknown property only with `strict`, else it is W116; at most 40 are listed); E003 duplicate id; E004 wall of zero length or shorter than its thickness (the second case is not raised while E011 reports a wrong scale); E005 opening refers to a missing wall; E006 opening does not fit its wall (`offset + width` beyond the wall length, with 1 mm of tolerance) or overlaps another opening on the same wall; E007 opening taller than the wall, or `sill + height` above the wall height; E008 room polygon has fewer than 3 distinct points, crosses itself, or has an area below 0.5 m2; E009 stair whose `to_level` names a missing level or its own level (a stair without `to_level` is valid); E010 a number that is not finite; E011 implausible scale after unit conversion (the larger side of the bounding box of all walls is below 2 m or above 200 m; the message suggests the likely correct `units`); E012 stair whose `from` and `to` are less than 0.2 m apart, or, without `to_level`, with a `rise` under 1 cm in absolute value; E013 an array above its cap (see Limits below): the plan is not read any further, and at most 10 such arrays are listed; E014 a wall end, room corner, stair end or furniture centre more than 1000 m from the origin after unit conversion (a mistyped number; not raised while E011 reports a wrong scale; the fix says to look for an extra digit in that point or a wrong `units`, or to subtract the offset when the whole plan only sits far from the origin).

Warning codes:
- W101 room boundary not backed by walls: more than 30% of the perimeter of the polygon is not within a wall's thickness plus 2 cm of any wall centerline (not raised while E011 reports a wrong scale, or when a wall, an opening or a level was left out); W102 declared `area` matches neither the polygon along the wall centerlines (within 5%) nor the polygon net of its walls (within 10%; every edge that runs along a wall moves inward by half that wall's thickness, an edge with no wall stays), because printed room areas are net; W103 two rooms on a level overlap by more than 5% of the smaller one; W104 door width outside 0.6 to 1.2 m; W105 a room cannot be reached from an entrance through doors, passages, stairs and open edges (an entrance is a room behind a door in an exterior wall, or the room around the top of a stair without `to_level` and with a negative `rise`; at least 0.7 m of shared boundary with no wall along it counts as an open edge, so a wall that was forgotten between two rooms is not reported here: compare the model with the drawing); W106 wall end that lies within 0.5 m of another wall without touching it (a corner that probably should close; a free end farther away, for example a partition that stops at a column, is normal); W107 room area below 2 m2 or above 80 m2 (not raised while E011 reports a wrong scale); W108 no door on an exterior wall (not raised when the lowest level has an `elevation` above 0.5 m, or when a stair without `to_level` and with a negative `rise` arrives from below: an upper floor is entered by its stair); W109 furniture whose centre lies outside every room on its level; W110 level without rooms; W111 the plan is so large or so tangled that some checks were skipped (see Limits); W112 furniture with a width, depth or height above 6 m (usually centimetres written as metres; the message gives the metre values), or longer than twice the span of its room; W113 level `height` outside 2 to 6 m; W114 stair `width` outside 0.5 to 3 m; W115 window `width` outside 0.3 to 4 m; W116 a property the schema does not know (the property is ignored and left out of `normalized`; `$schema` and keys that start with `x-` are allowed silently, and with `strict` it is error E002 instead). W112 to W115 are not raised while E011 reports a wrong scale.

**Limits.** The caps are one object, `CAPS` in `src/core/caps.js`; the array caps are mirrored as `maxItems` in `schema/plan.v1.schema.json` and a test (`tests/core/hostile.test.mjs`) keeps both equal. A plan over a cap gets E013 and nothing else: no array is walked past its cap, so a file with 100,000 walls is rejected in milliseconds.

| What | Cap |
| --- | --- |
| `levels` | 12 |
| `walls`, `openings` per level | 600 each |
| `rooms` per level | 150 |
| `stairs` per level | 24 |
| `furniture` per level | 400 |
| points of one room `polygon` | 120 |
| distance of any wall end, room corner, stair end or furniture centre from the origin | 1000 m (E014) |
| ids | 1 to 40 characters (letters, digits, `_`, `-`, `.`) |
| `name` of a level or room | 80 characters (plan `name`: 120) |
| `meta.scale_note`, `meta.notes`, `meta.generator` | 1000, 2000, 200 characters |
| `steps` of a stair | 2 to 40 |
| size of one furniture dimension (W112 above it) | 6 m |

Other bounds: a message repeats a value from the plan in at most 80 characters, and a message or fix is cut at 1500. At most 100 issues of one code are listed (the last says how many were left out). At most 1000 schema violations are collected, and beyond that the plan is not interpreted. Unknown properties (W116) have a separate limit of 1000: beyond it they are left out of the list and the plan is still read. Suggestions ("did you mean") are made only for words up to 48 characters, within a fixed work budget. The pairwise and sampling checks (room overlaps, walls behind room edges, net areas, reachability) share a budget of 12 million steps; a plan that uses it up gets W111 and the checks that did not run are left out. Keys that every object has (`__proto__`, `constructor`, `toString`) are unknown keys, and unlike other unknown keys they stay errors (E002) in the tolerant mode too.

**Command line.** `node tools/validate.mjs plan.json [--json] [--strict]` (also `npm run validate -- plan.json`; `-` reads the plan from standard input, `--help` prints the usage). Text output is one issue per line: `E006 levels[0].openings[3] "d-hall-bath": ... Fix: ...` (at most 15 lines per code, then one line that says how many more there are), then a summary line `N errors, M warnings`, then, when elements would be left out of the model, one more line that says how many (or that nothing usable would be left, so no model could be built). Unknown properties are warnings (W116); `--strict` makes them errors (E002). A chat answer is read the same way as in `analyzePlan`, and every change made to read it is printed first, one `note:` line each (for example `note: took the code block`). `--json` prints `{ ok, partial, errors, warnings, skipped, repairs }`. Exit code 0 when ok (and with `--strict` also no warnings and no repairs), 1 when not (a plan that would be built without some of its elements is not ok), 2 on a usage error or an unreadable file.

## 5. Test plan generator (tools/gen-plans.mjs)

Deterministic synthetic apartments for tests and demos: `node tools/gen-plans.mjs --seed N --count K --out DIR [--family NAME]`.

- `--seed N` is the first seed (default 1) and `--count K` the number of plans (default 7); plan number `i` uses seed `N + i`. `--out DIR` is required.
- Families: `studio`, `one-bedroom`, `two-bedroom` (with a corridor), `three-bedroom`, `l-shaped`, `balcony` (a one bedroom flat with a balcony) and `duplex` (a two level house with a straight stair). `--family NAME` fixes the family; without it the family follows the seed: seed 1 is `studio`, seed 2 `one-bedroom`, and so on in the order above, with seed 8 `studio` again. An unknown family stops the tool with an error.
- Output: one file `DIR/truth/<seed>-<family>.plan.json` per plan, with the seed padded to three digits (for example `001-studio.plan.json`); the folder is created, and one line `<seed>-<family>` per plan is printed. Without `--out`, or with a `--seed` or `--count` that is not a number, the tool prints a usage line and exits with code 2.
- The same options give byte identical files. Plans are in metres with `meta.source` set to `synthetic`, built from horizontal bands of rooms, with doors and windows placed from the geometry so that every room is reachable from the entrance. They validate without errors: `tests/render/plans14.test.mjs` checks the two examples and seeds 401 to 412, which cover all seven families.
- The module also exports `FAMILIES` and `generatePlan(seed, family?)`, which returns `{ family, plan }`; the tests and `tools/measure.mjs` and `tools/layout-svg.mjs` use it.
